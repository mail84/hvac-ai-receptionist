## Platform

web

## Users

Primary user: **the owner or operator of a small HVAC business**, typically a local crew of roughly 3–10 technicians.

Their situation when they reach this site: they are running a service business where the phone is the primary revenue channel, and calls are going unanswered — after hours, during overflow, or while techs are on a job and nobody is at a desk. They are evaluating whether to buy software that answers those calls.

The job they are doing on this site is a **purchase evaluation**, not a repair request. They are deciding whether this product is credible, whether it will work with how their business already runs, and whether it is worth a conversation.

Explicitly *not* the audience: homeowners or commercial clients seeking HVAC service. This is a B2B SaaS site whose visitors are HVAC business owners.

## Product Purpose

An AI phone receptionist for HVAC businesses. It answers inbound calls the business is currently missing and turns them into booked jobs.

Success for the business owner is measured in recovered revenue: calls that previously hit voicemail and were lost to a competitor instead become scheduled work.

## Positioning

Three confirmed mechanisms, which function together rather than as separate features:

1. **Captures missed calls** — answers what currently goes to voicemail: after-hours, overflow, and calls placed while technicians are working.
2. **Books into the calendar** — does not merely take a message. It writes the job into the business's existing scheduling system.
3. **Triages emergency versus routine** — understands HVAC context well enough to distinguish an urgent call from a routine one and route it accordingly.

The defensible position is the combination: a generic answering service or voicemail transcription tool captures the call but cannot book it or judge its urgency. HVAC-specific triage plus direct calendar write-through is the claim a neighboring product could not truthfully copy.

## Operating Context

The buyer's world, which future work must not misrepresent:

- Revenue arrives by phone. A missed call is usually a lost job, because the caller moves to the next search result.
- Demand is weather-driven and spiky. Heat waves and cold snaps produce call volume no small office can absorb.
- Urgency is genuinely tiered — a loss of heat or cooling is materially different from a maintenance request.
- The owner is frequently in the field, not at a desk, and is often evaluating this on a phone between jobs.
- The business already runs on some field-service scheduling software; this product is additive to that stack, not a replacement for it.

## Product Principles

1. **The buyer is not the caller.** Every surface addresses an HVAC owner making a purchase decision. Homeowner-facing framing is a category error.
2. **Lost revenue is the frame, not technology.** The product is sold on jobs recovered, not on the sophistication of the AI. Lead with the missed call, not the model.
3. **Fits the existing stack.** The buyer already has scheduling software and will not replace it. The product proves it works with how the business already operates.
4. **Claims stay inside what is confirmed.** This category invites invented metrics and fake logos. Every number, integration, and testimonial traces to something real, or it does not ship.
5. **Assume a phone, mid-job.** The owner evaluates in poor conditions and short windows. Comprehension has to survive that.
